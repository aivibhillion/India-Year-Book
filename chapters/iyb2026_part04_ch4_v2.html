Defence Highlights: Production, Exports & Budget

Record Defence Production: Annual defence production reached a record ₹1.51 lakh crore in FY 2024-25 — 18% growth over FY 2023-24 (₹1.27 lakh crore), and 90% up from FY 2019-20 (₹79,071 crore). DPSUs/other PSUs contributed ~77%, private sector 23% (up from 21% the previous year). Target: ₹3 lakh crore by 2029.

All-Time High Defence Exports: Touched ₹23,622 crore in FY 2024-25 — a 12.04% growth (₹2,539 crore) over FY 2023-24. Private sector contributed ₹15,233 crore, DPSUs ₹8,389 crore. Export target for 2029: ₹50,000 crore.

Record Defence Budget: MoD allocated ₹6.81 lakh crore in Union Budget 2025-26 — 9.53% more than FY 2024-25 BE, and 13.45% of the Union Budget (highest among all Ministries). Capital Outlay: ₹1.80 lakh crore; ~75% of modernisation budget (₹1.12 lakh crore) earmarked for domestic procurement, with 25% of that (~₹28,000 crore) reserved for domestic private industry. Capital contracts worth ₹1.82 lakh crore signed by Dec 2025.

Word Bank
Capital Outlay
— government spending on long-term assets (equipment, infrastructure), as opposed to "revenue" spending on day-to-day running costs like salaries.
DPSU
— Defence Public Sector Undertaking, a government-owned company producing defence equipment (e.g., HAL, BEL).

Indigenous Development: AMCA & New Technologies

Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA): The AMCA Programme Execution Model gives equal opportunity to private and public sectors on a competitive basis; bidders must be Indian companies (independently, JV, or consortia) — a major milestone towards Aatmanirbharta in the aerospace sector.

3rd LCA Mk1A / 2nd HTT-40 Production Line: Defence Minister inaugurated the 3rd LCA Tejas Mk1A production line and 2nd HTT-40 line at HAL's Nashik facility on 17 October 2025. HAL operationalised the 3rd line in a record two years; it can produce 8 aircraft/year, taking HAL's total capacity to 24 aircraft/year.

New Technologies:

  • Mission SWAYAT: Autonomous Weapon System (AWS) experiment conducted 23–25 Apr 2025 at Babina Field Firing Range to evaluate indigenous AWS capability.
  • Drone manufacturing: 819 drones built by EME units to date (193 Surveillance, 337 Kamikaze/Armed, 289 First Person View).
  • Mehar Baba Competition (MBC): IAF's UAV/swarm-drone innovation contest; winners of MBC-I got orders worth ₹800 crore from the three Services; 2nd edition concluded 29 July 2024. Aims to make India a Drone Hub by 2030.

Defence Acquisition, Atmanirbharta & Major Contracts

Defence Acquisition Council (DAC): chaired by the Defence Minister, has approved capital acquisition proposals worth over ₹3.84 lakh crore since January 2025 — covering RPAs, Loiter Munitions, Nag Missile System Mk-II, BrahMos launchers, Astra Mk-II, AEW&C systems and more.

DAP-2020 Review & DPM-2025: 2025 declared the "Year of Reforms" — DAP-2020 under review to promote JVs/ToT for private sector, FDI, and Defence Lines of Credit (Bharat Maitri Shakti). Defence Procurement Manual (DPM) 2025 released 23 Oct 2025 (effective 1 Nov 2025), facilitating ~₹1 lakh crore of revenue procurement.

Word Bank
ToT (Transfer of Technology)
— when a foreign supplier shares its technical know-how so an Indian company can manufacture the equipment domestically.
Indigenous content
— the percentage of a weapon system's parts/technology made within India rather than imported.
JV (Joint Venture) / Consortia
— a JV is a company formed by two or more firms sharing ownership; a consortium is a group of companies collaborating on one project without forming a new joint company.

Key Events of 2025

MiG-21 Decommissioning: IAF's legendary fighter, after more than six decades of service, was decommissioned in Chandigarh on 26 September 2025 — a ceremonial switch-off of six MiG-21 aircraft marked the occasion.

Operation Sagar Bandhu: Indian Armed Forces provided HADR to Cyclone Ditwah-hit Sri Lanka. INS Vikrant & INS Udaygiri delivered aid at Colombo; by 9 Dec 2025, IAF evacuated 424 Indian nationals, delivered 335 tons of medicines/relief material, airlifted 57 Sri Lankan Army troops; 10 IAF heavy-lift aircraft deployed a Field Hospital (treated 2,200+ patients) plus an Army Engineer Task Force. 7 Indian Naval ships delivered 1,044 tons of relief material in total.

President's Sorties: On 29 Oct 2025, President Droupadi Murmu flew a Rafale sortie at Ambala — first President to fly two IAF fighter types (having flown a Su-30 MKI in 2023). On 28 Dec 2025, she took a submarine sortie aboard INS Vaghsheer — only the second President to do so after Dr APJ Abdul Kalam.

UN Peacekeeping & Women in Armed Forces

India, a founding UN member, has participated since 1950 (UN Multinational Force, Korea) in 53 of 72 UN peacekeeping missions, contributing over 2,97,000 personnel — one of the largest peacekeeping contributors globally. Currently the 4th largest troop contributor: 5,200+ troops in 12 missions (DR Congo, South Sudan, Abyei, Lebanon, Golan Heights). Indian Army's Centre for UN Peacekeeping (CUNPK), New Delhi, trains 10,000+ troops/year including ~2,000 foreign personnel.

Women in the Forces: India was among the first to deploy an all-female Police Unit to a UN mission (Liberia); 59 women officers currently deployed in UN operations. In the Army: ~8,300 women officers, 507 given Permanent Commission, 124 in command appointments, 10 women combat aviators, women enrolled in Corps of Military Police since 2019. Navy: all branches except submarines open to women, 750+ women officers. IAF: women in all branches/combat roles since 2022 scheme, 19 women fighter pilots by end-2024 — IAF is the only Service offering women equal opportunity in all combat roles. NDA: women's entry since 2022, first batch of 17 passed out May 2025. 10% seats reserved for girls in all 33 Sainik Schools from 2021-22.

Word Bank
Permanent Commission
— a career appointment that lasts until retirement, with eligibility for promotion and pension, as opposed to a Short Service Commission with a fixed, shorter tenure.

Department of Military Affairs & Reforms

Creation of DMA and appointment of the Chief of Defence Staff (CDS) in 2019 is the most significant recent defence reform — promotes jointness in procurement/training/staffing and restructuring of military commands.

Key 2025 reforms:

  • Defence Space Agency: Space-Based Surveillance (SBS-3) programme — 52 satellites (21 ISRO + 31 private industry), cost ₹25,221.75 crore, cost-shared by Army/Navy/AF (20% each), NTRO/ARC (15% each), MHA (10%); approved by CCS Nov 2024. Conducted first tri-services space exercise "Antariksha Abhyas-2024."
  • Defence Cyber Agency (DCyA): Established CERT & Cyber Forensics Labs (Meity-accredited); successfully defended Tri-Services networks during Op Sindoor with zero cyber breaches.
  • Technology Perspective and Capability Roadmap (TPCR)-2025: MoD's 3rd public capability roadmap (after 2013, 2018), covering 15 years; released at "Ran Samwad" seminar, Mhow, 27 Aug 2025.
  • Armed Forces Vision 2047 (Part-1): approved by CDS & Defence Minister, aligned with Vision India 2047.
  • Inter-Services Organisation (Command, Control & Discipline) Act, 2023: empowers C-in-C/Officer-in-Command of ISOs with disciplinary/administrative powers (previously governed separately by Army Act 1950, Navy Act 1957, Air Force Act 1950).

Indian Army

Northern Front: Stable yet sensitive; after Depsang/Demchok disengagement (2024), 2025 saw reduced PLA deployment (10 Combined Arms Brigades opposite Northern borders). New force multipliers deployed: Rudra Brigades, Divyastra Batteries, Bhairav Battalions.

J&K: Security situation firmly under control ("Whole of Nation Approach") — zero stone-pelting incidents; but 791 drone intrusions reported (9 along J&K IB, 782 along Punjab/Rajasthan IB); 237 drones brought down by own forces.

North East: Stable, improving — since Jan 2025, 26 insurgents neutralised, 1,024 apprehended, 111 surrendered, 542 weapons recovered. Manipur: President's Rule from 13 Feb 2025 improved the situation.

HADR: Assistance provided across 10 states — 8,092 civilians rescued, medical aid to ~7,159, relief material to 2,617+.

Military Diplomacy: Engaged with 118 countries; 39 joint exercises.

Indian Air Force

Demonstrated all-round capability during Op Sindoor. New Platforms: C-295 MW aircraft from Airbus (two squadrons equipped; first domestically-made C-295 due 2026); indigenous Counter-UAV System inducted.

Air Defence: Integrated Air Command and Control System fused surveillance picture, shared with Army (via Akashteer) and Navy (via IMSAS) — ensured effective air defence across the Western front.

Anti-Naxal Ops: South Bastar (21 Apr–10 May 2025) — 2,609 troops, 158 tons load, 18 casualties airlifted; supported elimination of 31 Naxals.

HADR: Dima Hasao mine rescue (Assam, Jan 2025); Mana avalanche relief (Uttarakhand, Feb 2025); evacuation from Maesot Thailand (Mar 2025), Jordan/Egypt (Iran-Israel war, Jun 2025); Myanmar earthquake relief — 177 tons material, 198 personnel.

Defence Cooperation: Establishing six new defence wings abroad, starting with Spain, Armenia, Ivory Coast.

Indian Navy

Commissioned 12 ships/submarines in 2025 including INS Surat (4th & last P15B), INS Nilgiri/Udaygiri/Himgiri (P17A stealth frigates), INS Vaghsheer (6th & last P75 submarine), INS Tamal (from Russia), INS Ikshak (Survey Vessel Large). Navy's proactive deployment during Op Sindoor confined the Pakistan Navy fleet close to its shores. Indian Maritime Doctrine revised & publicly released.

Warship Construction: 177+ ships/submarines built in India to date; all 52 planned warships being built domestically; 75 more ships/submarines in final approval stages.

Maritime Security: Ensured safe passage of Indian Flag Merchant Vessels through the Strait of Hormuz and Gulf of Aden/Red Sea amid Israel-Hamas conflict & Somalia piracy re-emergence.

Indian Coast Guard & Ranks of the Three Services

Since inception (1977), ICG has grown to 152 ships, 78 aircraft, 42 stations, 11 Air stations/enclaves, 84 Radar stations — one of the largest coast guards in the world.

Coastal Security: anti-poaching ops → 10 foreign boats & 128 poachers arrested; anti-smuggling → 14 boats, 68 smugglers arrested, ₹37,850.03 crore contraband seized; 140 lives saved in SAR missions. Notable ops: MSC Elsa 3 sinking off Kochi (24 crew rescued, May 2025) and MV Wan Hai 503 fire off Kerala coast (Jun 2025).

Recruitment & Territorial Army

Agnipath Scheme: recruits youth as Agniveers for 4 years; up to 25% retained in regular cadre after that. First batch's tenure ends Dec 2026. MHA has mandated 10% horizontal reservation in CAPFs; Railways 10% in RPF; MoD 10% across all levels; various loan schemes (Mudra, Stand Up India) support Agniveers post-service. Age relaxation: 5 years for first batch, 3 years for later batches.

Officer Entry: mainly via UPSC — NDA/Naval Academy exam and Combined Defence Services Examination (CDSE), twice yearly. Non-UPSC entry via Services Selection Boards (SSBs) for Engineering/Technical/Legal streams, Short Service Commission (Women), NCC & University Entry Scheme.

Territorial Army (TA): raised by C Rajagopalachari in 1949 — provides part-time military training to employed citizens (2 months annual training). Includes unique unit types: Infantry, Ecological, Railways, ONGC, IOCL, Engineers. 9 Ecological Task Force units + 1 River rejuvenation unit engaged in eco-restoration and Ganga/Gomti rejuvenation.

Word Bank
Agniveer
— a soldier recruited under the Agnipath scheme for a fixed 4-year tenure, with only a portion retained afterward.
JCO / OR
— Junior Commissioned Officer and Other Ranks; non-gazetted Army personnel ranks below commissioned officers.

Defence Production, DPSUs & Private Sector/FDI

Department of Defence Production (DDP), set up in 1962, develops the industrial base for defence equipment through erstwhile Ordnance Factories and DPSUs, guided by the Defence Production Policy for self-reliance.

DPSUs: Ordnance Factory Board (41 production units) converted into 7 new DPSUs from 2021 — Munitions India Ltd., Armoured Vehicles Nigam Ltd., Advanced Weapons and Equipment India Ltd., Troops Comforts Ltd., Yantra India Ltd., India Optel Ltd., Gliders India Ltd. Other DPSUs: HAL, Bharat Electronics Ltd., Bharat Dynamics Ltd., Garden Reach Shipbuilders, Goa Shipyard, Hindustan Shipyard, Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders, Mishra Dhatu Nigam.

Private Sector & FDI: Opened for 100% Indian private participation + 26% FDI in May 2001 (subject to licensing). Pre-2013-14: 195 licenses issued; since 2013-14: 576 licenses issued. Cumulative FDI inflow (May 2001–Jun 2025): ₹5,730.55 crore; from Apr 2014–Jun 2025: ₹4,342.13 crore. 47 JV FDI proposals approved.

DPSU Bhawan inaugurated 10 Nov 2025 at World Trade Centre, New Delhi — a common platform for all 16 DPSUs.

Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO)

DRDO is MoD's R&D wing — lead designer of fighter/reconnaissance aircraft, armoured vehicles, missiles, radars, underwater and soldier-support systems. Flagship systems: BrahMos, Akash, VSHORADS, ASAT Missile, Directed Energy Weapon (DEW), LCA Tejas, AEW&C, Arjun MBT, Pinaka, ATAGS, Varunastra Torpedo, and more.

Support to industry: Technology Development Fund (TDF) grants up to ₹50 crore; 15 DRDO Industry Academia Centres of Excellence (DIA-CoEs) at IITs/IISc; test facilities opened to private industry; free-access Patent Policy — 2,100+ free Transfer of Technologies for 132+ systems.

2025 Achievements: Pinaka Long Range Guided Rocket (120 km, 29 Dec); salvo launch of 2 Pralay missiles (31 Dec); ASTRA BVRAAM test from LCA (12 Mar); Medium-Range SAM Army-version flight tests (3-4 Apr); LRGB "Gaurav" glide bomb trials (~100 km range, Apr 8-10); India's first long-range hypersonic missile trial (16 Nov 2024); Akash Prime destroyed 2 aerial targets in Ladakh; Agni-Prime rail-mobile-launcher test (24 Sep 2025).

Foreign collaboration: co-chairs India-USA Joint Technology Group, Indo-Israel Management Council, India-Russia R&D Subgroup, and steering committees with Singapore, UK, Korea.

Border Roads Organisation (BRO)

Functions under MoD building infrastructure for MoRTH/MHA/MEA in border states & Bhutan. Expanded from 2 Projects to presence across 11 states, 3 UTs and Bhutan, with 18 Projects currently. Budget hit a record ₹16,690 crore in FY 2024-25; target ₹18,700 crore for FY 2025-26.

Landmark projects: Sela Tunnel (Arunachal, inaugurated Mar 2024, highest tunnel in India at 13,000 ft); Shinkhun La Tunnel (under construction, will be world's highest at 15,800 ft); Zojila Pass reopened in a record 32 days (Apr 2025); Brangsa Bridge — world's highest multi-span bridge (14,900 ft, built in 174 days, recognised by World Book of Records); Mudh-Nyoma Airfield (13,710 ft, Eastern Ladakh, completing 2026).

On 7 Dec 2025, Defence Minister dedicated 125 projects simultaneously (28 roads, 93 bridges, 4 misc.) across Ladakh, J&K and 7 states — the highest-value inauguration in BRO's history (~₹5,000 crore). BRO also executes Indo-Myanmar Border Fencing (1,643 km across 4 states) and LWE-affected road projects in Chhattisgarh.

Sainik Schools, NCC & Resettlement of Ex-Servicemen

Sainik School Society: 33 Sainik Schools currently functioning; 34th (Bhind, MP) and 35th (Langvoku, Assam) approved. 86 Private/NGO/State schools given provisional approval under the "100 new schools" scheme.

National Cadet Corps (NCC): established under the NCC Act, 1945; strength targeted to reach 20 lakh by 2047. Girl cadet enrolment up from 25.4% (2014) to 40% (2025). 10 cadets completed a Mt Everest expedition (May 2025); ~75,000 cadets participated in relief work during Operation Sindoor.
