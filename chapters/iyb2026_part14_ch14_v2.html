Ministry of Education & RTE Act, 2009

Education was originally a State subject; moved to the Concurrent List by the 42nd Amendment, 1976 (Entry 25, List III) — central law prevails on conflict. Ministry of Education has 2 departments: School Education and Literacy, and Higher Education.

RTE Act, 2009 — effective 1 April 2010; 86th Amendment (2002) added Article 21-A making free/compulsory education a Fundamental Right (ages 6-14). Mandates unaided private schools (non-minority) reserve 25% seats at entry level for weaker sections. Amended in 2012, 2017 (teacher qualifications), 2019 (assessment/detention), 2022.

National Education Policy (NEP) 2020

Replaces the New Education Policy of 1986. Structural shift: 5+3+3+4 curricular framework, backed by NCF-SE 2023 & NCF for Foundational Stage. Assessment reforms: shift from high-stakes exams to formative, competency-based evaluation. Higher education: multiple entry/exit options + Academic Bank of Credit; separates regulatory/accreditation/standard-setting functions.

SARTHAQ — Student and Teacher's Holistic Advancement Through Quality Education; the detailed implementation roadmap operationalising NEP 2020.

Word Bank
5+3+3+4 structure
— NEP 2020's new school stage breakdown: 5 years Foundational (ages 3-8), 3 years Preparatory, 3 years Middle, 4 years Secondary — replacing the old 10+2 structure.
Academic Bank of Credit
— a digital system storing academic credits earned across institutions/programmes, letting students transfer/accumulate credits flexibly, similar to a bank account.

NISHTHA, DIKSHA & Foundational Learning

NISHTHA — teacher capacity-building programme, launched 2019; 12 lakh+ teachers trained. Online NISHTHA on DIKSHA platform from 2021 (COVID-response), self-paced with certification.

Other initiatives: NIPUN Bharat & Vidya Pravesh — foundational literacy/numeracy, reaching 4.2 crore+ students across 8.9 lakh schools; PRASHAST app — disability screening; PARAKH & Holistic Progress Cards — formative assessment; Samagra Shiksha — integrated school education scheme; 72%+ schools now have internet connectivity.

PM Schools for Rising India (PM SHRI)

Centrally sponsored scheme transforming schools into NEP 2020 exemplar institutions; developing 14,500+ PM SHRI Schools from Central/State/UT/Local Body/KVS/NVS-managed schools. Implementation period: 2022-23 to 2026-27. Focus: hands-on learning, modern labs/libraries/ICT infrastructure.

PM POSHAN (Mid-Day Meal)

Rights-based CSS under the National Food Security Act, 2013; covers Bal Vatika + Classes I-VIII in govt/govt-aided schools. Tithi Bhojan — community food-sharing initiative (originated in Gujarat, adopted nationally from 2018). Quality checks via NABL/FSSAI/CSIR-accredited labs; Mid-Day Meal Rules (2015) mandate food testing.

National Means-cum-Merit Scholarship Scheme (NMMSS)

Launched 2008 — reduces dropout among economically weaker meritorious students. 1 lakh fresh scholarships/year for Class IX students, renewable through Class XII. Eligibility: parental income ≤₹3.50 lakh/year, min 55% marks in Class VII (5% relaxation for SC/ST).

Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan & Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti

KVS — approved 1962 for transferable central govt employees' children; registered as a society 1965. As of Jun 2025: 1,288 Kendriya Vidyalayas, incl. 3 abroad (Kathmandu, Moscow, Tehran).

NVS — envisaged under the National Policy on Education, 1986; fully residential, co-educational, CBSE-affiliated schools for talented rural children regardless of socio-economic background.

NCERT & CBSE

NCERT — established 1961; apex advisory body on school/teacher education; develops 4 National Curriculum Frameworks (school ed., teacher ed., early childhood, adult ed.). NCF-Foundational Stage (2022) & NCF-School Education (2023) operationalise the 5+3+3+4 structure.

CBSE — national school board; serves schools in 26 foreign countries too. Conducts Class X/XII exams; transitioning to competency-based evaluation. SAFAL programme evaluates learning at grades 3, 5, 8. Also serves as Standards Setting Authority for KV/NV schools.

NIOS & National Bal Bhavan

NIOS — one of the world's largest open schooling systems; ~10,000 study centres + 22 regional centres. DEEP (e-library, 2022) offers 75 subjects, 12,000+ e-books. New courses under "Bhartiya Jnana Parampara" (Veda Adhyayan, Sanskrit Vyakaran).

National Bal Bhavan — established 1956; non-formal education for ages 5-16; ~140 Bal Bhavans/Kendras nationwide + 37 Bal Kendras in NCT.

Vidyanjali, Inclusive Education & ULLAS

Vidyanjali (2021) — school volunteer management portal connecting schools with volunteers/CSR contributors.

Inclusive Education — Samagra Shiksha covers Children with Special Needs (CwSN) per the RPwD Act, 2016.

ULLAS – Nav Bharat Saaksharta Karyakram — adult literacy CSS for non-literates aged 15+; 5 components (foundational literacy, life skills, basic education, vocational skills, continuing education); delivered via Samajik Chetna Kendras.

Unnat Bharat Abhiyan & National Digital Library

Unnat Bharat Abhiyan (UBA) — launched 2014; connects universities with rural communities; extended to March 2026.

National Digital Library of India (NDLI) — centralised digital repository of educational resources with remote access.

Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan

Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan Bill, 2025 — approved by Union Cabinet; proposes a single unified regulator for higher education, repealing the UGC Act 1956, AICTE Act 1987, and NCTE Act 1993. Structure: 3 councils — Regulatory, Accreditation, Standards. Emphasises faceless, technology-enabled, trust-based regulation via a single-window digital disclosure system.

Higher & Technical Education (AISHE, NIRF)

At Independence: 20 universities, 500 colleges. Per AISHE: India now has 1,168+ universities, 45,473 colleges, 12,002 standalone institutions. GER (18-23 age): ~28.4% (female GER slightly higher at 28.5%).

NIRF (National Institutional Ranking Framework) — launched 2015; ranks institutions on teaching, research, graduation outcomes, outreach, inclusivity, perception.

Other Higher Education Initiatives & Scholarships

Initiatives: World Class Institutions Scheme, Malaviya Mission (teacher training), Research Parks, NATS (National Apprenticeship Training Scheme), PMRF (PM's Research Fellows), GIAN, Study in India programme.

Scholarships: SAKSHAM (differently-abled), Pragati (girl students), Swami Vivekananda Scholarship (single girl child), Ishan Vikas (North-East students). SWAYAM — free online courses platform. B.Voc under NSQF & SAMVAY — integrate vocational training with academics.

Online Safety and Child Protection

IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 — mandate diligence & grievance mechanisms on digital platforms. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 — requires parental consent for processing children's data; bans targeted ads to children. PRAGYATA Guidelines (Ministry of Education) — recommend age-appropriate screen time.

Copyright

Copyright arises automatically on creation (no formality needed) under the Copyright Act, 1957; registration (not mandatory) is prima facie evidence of ownership (Section 48). Copyright Office established 1958, under Department of Higher Education. Section 13 covers literary/dramatic/musical/artistic works, including software, films, sound recordings.

Copyright Board (constituted 1958) — quasi-judicial body, historically adjudicated registration/assignment disputes and compulsory licences; also fixed statutory royalty rates. e-filing introduced 2014.

Word Bank
Prima facie evidence
— evidence accepted as sufficient proof unless disproved; here, a copyright registration is treated as valid ownership proof unless someone successfully challenges it.
Compulsory licence
— a licence a government can grant to use copyrighted material without the owner's consent, under specific public-interest circumstances (e.g., translation, access for persons with disabilities).
