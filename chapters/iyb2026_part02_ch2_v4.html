National Symbols

pp. 28–31

Timeline of National Symbols

National Flag

Tricolour: saffron (top), white (middle, with navy-blue 24-spoke Ashoka Chakra), green (bottom). Must be made of hand-spun/handwoven Khadi cotton/silk per the Flag Code; MHA directs states/UTs against plastic flags (not biodegradable). Paper flags permitted at events under the Flag Code of India, 2002 but must be disposed of privately/with dignity, not discarded on the ground.

Word Bank
Khadi
— hand-spun, hand-woven cloth (usually cotton, silk or wool) made popular during India's freedom struggle as a symbol of self-reliance; the National Flag must legally be made of it.
Flag Code
— the official set of rules in India governing how the National Flag may be displayed, used, and disposed of with dignity.
Biodegradable
— material that naturally breaks down and decomposes over time (like cloth/paper); plastic is NOT biodegradable, which is why plastic flags are discouraged.

State Emblem

Adaptation of the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath. Original: 4 lions back-to-back on a circular abacus with elephant, galloping horse, bull, lion (separated by Dharma Chakras) on the frieze, resting on a bell-shaped lotus.

State Emblem (adopted 26 January 1950): shows only 3 lions visible, Dharma Chakra in centre, bull (right), galloping horse (left), outline Chakras at extreme ends; bell-shaped lotus omitted. Motto: "Satyameva Jayate" (Truth alone Triumphs, Devanagari, from the Mundaka Upanishad) inscribed below.

Regulated by the State Emblem of India (Prohibition of Improper Use) Act, 2005 and the State Emblem of India (Regulation of Use) Rules, 2007 (amended 2010).

Word Bank
Abacus
— here, the flat circular platform/slab at the top of the pillar on which the four lions stand (an architectural term, not the counting tool).
Frieze
— a decorated horizontal band, usually carved with figures or patterns, running around a structure — here it's the carved band around the abacus showing the elephant, horse, bull and lion.
Dharma Chakra
— the "Wheel of Law/Righteousness," a Buddhist symbol representing the teachings of the Buddha; it also appears at the centre of the Indian National Flag.
Motto
— a short phrase expressing a guiding principle; India's national motto is "Satyameva Jayate" (Truth alone Triumphs).

National Anthem

Jana-gana-mana, composed in Bangla by Rabindranath Tagore; Hindi version adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 24 January 1950. First sung 27 December 1911 at the Calcutta session of the INC. Full song = 5 stanzas; first stanza is the official anthem. Playing time: ~52 seconds (full); short version = first & last lines, ~20 seconds.

Bharata-bhagya-vidhata
Panjaba-Sindhu-Gujarata-Maratha
Dravida-Utkala-Banga
Vindhya-Himachala-Yamuna-Ganga
uchchala-jaladhi-taranga
Tava subha name jage, Tave subha asisa mage,

Jana-gana-mangala-dayaka jaya he
Bharata-bhagya-vidhata.
Jaya he, jaya he, jaya he, Jaya jaya jaya, jaya he!
Word Bank
Stanza
— a group of lines forming a single unit/verse within a song or poem; the full anthem has 5 stanzas, but only the first is officially sung as the National Anthem.
Constituent Assembly
— the body that drafted and adopted India's Constitution (1946–1950); it also formally adopted the National Anthem.

National Song

Vande Mataram, composed in Sanskrit by Bankimchandra Chatterji; first published 1875; first sung on a political platform at the 1896 INC session. Composition date corroborated by Sri Aurobindo's writing (16 April 1907, in the daily Bande Mataram). 7 November 2025 marked its 150th anniversary. Status equal to the National Anthem. English rendering by Sri Aurobindo.

Word Bank
Corroborated
— confirmed or supported by additional independent evidence (here, Aurobindo's 1907 writing supports the song's 1875 composition date).
Rendering
— a translated or adapted version of a work in another language (Sri Aurobindo's English rendering = his English translation of the Sanskrit original).

National Calendar

Based on the Saka Era, first month Chaitra, normal year 365 days; adopted 22 March 1957 alongside the Gregorian calendar for: (i) Gazette of India, (ii) All India Radio news broadcasts, (iii) Government of India calendars, (iv) Government communications to the public. 1 Chaitra = 22 March normally, 21 March in a leap year.

Word Bank
Saka Era
— a traditional Indian calendar system starting in 78 CE, used as the basis of India's official National Calendar alongside the Gregorian (international) calendar.
Gregorian calendar
— the standard 12-month, 365/366-day calendar used internationally today (the one on your phone).
Leap year
— a year with one extra day (366 instead of 365) added to keep the calendar aligned with Earth's orbit; occurs roughly every 4 years.

National Animal & Bird

Tiger
National Animal
Peacock
National Bird

Both re-notified by the Wildlife Division, then-Ministry of Environment & Forests, notification dated 30 May 2011.

Note the wording: the book uses "re-notified" — a recurring exact-word trap in objective questions.
Word Bank
Notified / Re-notified
— "notified" means officially declared through a government gazette/order; "re-notified" means the same declaration was issued again (renewed/reconfirmed), not that the status changed.
Wildlife Division
— the branch of the (then) Ministry of Environment and Forests responsible for wildlife protection policy and notifications like this one.

Practice MCQs — Chapter 2

Score: 0/8
PYQ · APPSC Gr-2 2022Medium
Q1. The National Flag must be made of hand-spun/handwoven cloth of which material as per the Flag Code?
The Flag must be Khadi (hand-spun/handwoven) cotton or silk yarn cloth. Plastic flags are prohibited as they are non-biodegradable.
Medium
Q2. The State Emblem of India was adopted on:
The State Emblem was adopted on 26 January 1950 — the same date the Constitution came into force.
PYQ · UPSC Prelims 2021Hard
Q3. In the original Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath, how many lions are mounted on the abacus?
The original has 4 lions mounted back-to-back. The State Emblem shows only 3 visible in profile (the 4th is hidden behind); the bell-shaped lotus is also omitted in the Emblem version.
Medium
Q4. "Jana-gana-mana" was first sung at which session?
First sung on 27 December 1911 at the Calcutta Session of the Indian National Congress; adopted as National Anthem on 24 January 1950.
PYQ · TGPSC 2023Medium
Q5. The playing time of the full version of the National Anthem is approximately:
Full version ≈ 52 seconds; short version (first & last lines) ≈ 20 seconds.
Easy
Q6. Vande Mataram was composed by:
Composed in Sanskrit by Bankimchandra Chatterji; first published in 1875, first sung on a political platform at the 1896 INC session.
PYQ · SSC CGL 2022Medium
Q7. India's National Calendar (Saka Era) was adopted alongside the Gregorian calendar on:
Adopted 22 March 1957, first month Chaitra; 1 Chaitra = 22 March normally, 21 March in a leap year.
Medium
Q8. Tiger and Peacock were re-notified as National Animal and National Bird via a notification dated:
Re-notified by the Wildlife Division of the then-Ministry of Environment and Forests on 30 May 2011.
