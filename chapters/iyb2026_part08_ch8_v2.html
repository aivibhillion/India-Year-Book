Macro-Financial Snapshot

Credit & Banking: Repo rate cut by 100 bps (Feb-Jun 2025); WALR on fresh loans at 8.5% (Sep 2025). Gross NPAs at 2.3%, Net NPAs at 0.5% (Mar 2025) — lowest since 2011-12; PCR at 76.3%; CRAR 17.3%; CET-1 14.7%; RoA 1.4%.

Balance of Payments: Current Account Deficit 0.6% of GDP (2024-25), down from 0.7%. Merchandise trade deficit rose to USD 287.2 billion. Net FDI inflows: USD 29.6 billion.

Sovereign Green Bonds: ₹16,000 cr (FY23) → ₹20,000 cr (FY24) → ₹21,697 cr (FY25).

Forex Reserves: USD 696.61 billion (26 Dec 2025) — among top 5 globally; peak was USD 704.9 billion (Sep 2024).

GIFT IFSC & FRBM Framework

GIFT IFSC — operationalised in GIFT City, Gandhinagar, Gujarat, 2015; International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA) promotes FinTech via Regulatory & Innovation Sandboxes.

FRBM Act, 2003 (amended 2018) — mandates sustainable fiscal management; requires laying Medium-term Fiscal Policy, Fiscal Policy Strategy, Macro-economic Framework, and Medium-term Expenditure Framework statements in Parliament. Targets: Fiscal Deficit ≤3% of GDP (by 31 Mar 2021); General Govt Debt ≤60% GDP; Central Govt Debt ≤40% GDP (by 31 Mar 2025).

Infrastructure Financing: VGF, IIPDF & NMP

VGF Scheme (2006, revamped 2020 for social sector) — Viability Gap Funding for PPP projects; Centre provides up to 20% Capex (economic sector) or up to 30-40% for social/pilot sectors. 71 projects approved, Total Project Cost ₹46,157.27 cr, GoI VGF share ₹7,923.14 cr.

IIPDF Scheme (2007, restructured 2022) — funds project development/Transaction Adviser costs; 31 projects approved, ₹64.13 cr total cost.

National Monetisation Pipeline (NMP) — launched per Union Budget 2021-22; First pipeline (FY22-25) targeted ₹6 lakh crore, achieved ₹5.3 lakh crore monetised. NMP 2.0 (FY25-30), launched in Budget 2025-26, targets ₹10 lakh crore.

Infrastructure Debt Funds, InvITs & REITs

IDFs — refinance infra debt, enable bank lending to greenfield projects; operate as NBFCs/Mutual Funds. As of Mar 2025: 3 IDF-NBFCs (India Infradebt, Kotak IDF, NIIF IFL) managing over ₹52,000 crore.

NIIF & SWAMIH Investment Fund

NIIF Limited manages 4 funds: Master Fund (corpus ₹15,998 cr, core infra — roads/ports/airports/power; investors incl. ADIA, CPPIB, Temasek); Fund of Funds-I (₹4,281 cr); India-Japan Fund (launched Aug 2023, ₹4,900 cr, backed by GoI + JBIC); Strategic Opportunities Fund.

SWAMIH Investment Fund — established Dec 2019 (Category-II AIF) for stalled/mid-income housing; managed by SBICAP Ventures; total commitment ₹15,531 cr; as of Oct 2025, 160 investments made, ~58,596 housing units completed/near-completed.

Digital Economy & UPI Globalisation

DEA runs 3 FinTech Joint Working Groups (Singapore, Philippines, UK). UPI accepted in Bhutan, France (e-commerce), UAE, Singapore, Sri Lanka, Mauritius, Qatar, Nepal; UPI-PayNow linkage with Singapore for cross-border remittances. NPCI International has reached out to 80+ countries, executing MoUs with 20+ payment partners across 30+ countries.

Department of Expenditure & Power Sector Reforms

Department of Expenditure — implements Finance Commission/Pay Commission recommendations, monitors audits, prepares GoI Accounts, approves centrally-funded schemes.

Power Sector Reforms: states get additional borrowing of 0.5% of GSDP annually (linked to power-sector reform performance) — ~₹1,71,612 crore for FY 2025-26.

Pension Borrowing: states allowed extra borrowing = employer+employee NPS/UPS contributions (since FY22-23); ₹62,778 crore permitted for 2024-25.

SASCI (Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment) — 50-year interest-free loans; outlay grew from ₹12,000 cr (2020-21) to ₹1,50,000 cr (2025-26); total released till Sep 2025: ₹4,10,814.81 crore.

Public Financial Management System (PFMS) & DBT

PFMS enables digital payment/tracking to reduce leakages & ghost beneficiaries. 2024-25: ₹3.75 lakh crore paid under 1,169 DBT schemes (PM-KISAN, MGNREGA, PMAY, NSAP etc.); 650+ banks integrated.

Key initiatives: e-Bill (since Mar 2022, 523 Pay & Accounts Offices); SNA-SPARSH (just-in-time fund flow, all 28 states + 3 UTs, 66 CSS); Hybrid TSA (282 schemes, Hybrid TSA in 107).

Department of Financial Services (DFS)

DFS oversees Banking, Insurance, Pension sector reforms; manages PSBs/PSICs/PSFIs; top appointments (Governors, Deputy Governors, Regulators' Chairpersons); supervises RRBs & Cooperative Banks. Administers PMJDY, PMJJBY, PMSBY, APY, PMMY, Stand Up India for financial inclusion.

Word Bank
NPA (Non-Performing Asset)
— a loan on which the borrower has stopped making interest/principal payments for a set period (usually 90 days).
PCR (Provisioning Coverage Ratio)
— the percentage of expected loan losses a bank has already set aside as a financial cushion — higher is safer.
CRAR (Capital to Risk-Weighted Asset Ratio)
— a bank's capital compared to its risk-weighted loans, measuring its ability to absorb losses.
PCA (Prompt Corrective Action)
— an RBI framework restricting a weak bank's lending/expansion until its financial health improves.

NARCL & EASE Reforms

NARCL (with its AMC, IDRCL) — targets ~₹2 lakh crore in stressed assets; govt guarantee up to ₹30,600 crore for Security Receipts (5 years). As of Jun 2025: acquired 27 accounts (₹1,57,215 crore exposure), recovered ₹2,410 crore.

EASE Reforms (conceptualised 2015, launched 2017) — for PSB financial stability/NPA management/digital transformation, overseen by the EASE Steering Committee of IBA. Evolution: EASE 1.0-4.0 → EASENext (post PSB Manthan 2.0, 2022) → EASE 5.0-7.0 → EASE 8.0/EASE Rise (FY26) — focuses on Risk & Resilience, Innovation, Socio-economic Impact, Excellence.

Digital Banking Units & Debt Recovery Tribunals

DBUs — announced Budget 2022-23; 75 launched 16 Oct 2022 (Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav); as of Jul 2025, 114 DBUs operate in 110 districts via 27 banks.

DRTs — under the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act 1993 & SARFAESI Act 2002; currently 39 DRTs + 5 DRATs, handling debts above ₹20 lakh. Mandatory e-filing from 1 Feb 2023; document scrutiny time cut from 7 to 3 days; summons wait cut from 15 to 7 days.

e-DRT, BAANKNET, CKYCRR & Crisis Management

e-DRT (since 1 Oct 2018) digitised all DRTs/DRATs. BAANKNET — e-auction portal, relaunched 3 Jan 2025 (was e-Bkray, 2019). CKYCRR (since 2016, managed by CERSAI) — hosts 94+ crore KYC records.

Crisis Management Plan (CMP-2024) — activated for bank strikes lasting 3+ days. Staff Welfare Fund (revised 2024) — benefits 15 lakh staff across 12 PSBs.

Agriculture Credit: NABARD & KCC

NABARD (est. 1982) — 2024-25 refinance disbursements: ₹3,25,497.81 crore total. RIDF (since 1995-96): ₹5.94 lakh crore sanctioned cumulatively for 7.87 lakh projects. Ground Level Agriculture Credit FY25: ₹28.69 lakh crore disbursed vs ₹27.50 lakh crore target (104%) — quadrupled since FY14 at 13.06% CAGR.

Kisan Credit Card (KCC) — introduced 1998-99; interest subvention 1.5% on loans up to ₹3 lakh + 3% prompt-repayment incentive = effective 4% rate. As of Mar 2025: 7.72 crore KCC accounts operational, ₹10.20 lakh crore outstanding.

Pension Sector: PFRDA, APY, NPS & UPS

PFRDA Act — passed 19 Sep 2013, notified 1 Feb 2014; regulates NPS, APY, UPS.

APY (2015) — guaranteed monthly pension ₹1,000-5,000 for ages 18-40; as of Jul 2025: 8.03 crore enrolments, 47.83% female subscribers.

NPS — mandatory for central govt employees from 1 Jan 2004; opened to all citizens from May 2009; active PRANs at 66.40 lakh (Mar 2025).

UPS (effective 1 April 2025) — optional scheme under NPS; assured payout of 50% of average basic pay (last 12 months) after 25 years' service; minimum guaranteed payout ₹10,000/month after 10 years; employee contributes 10% of Basic+DA, matched by govt + additional 8.5% to Pool Corpus.

Flagship Schemes: PMJDY & Social Security

PMJDY — announced 15 Aug 2014, launched 28 Aug 2014; Phase I (2014-15): basic accounts + RuPay card (₹1 lakh accident insurance); Phase II (2015-18): overdraft up to ₹5,000. As of Jul 2025: 56.04 crore accounts, deposits ₹2,60,859 crore, 56% held by women.

Department of Public Enterprises & NLMC

DPE — as of 1 Apr 2025: 14 Maharatna, 26 Navratna, 53 Miniratna Category-I, 10 Miniratna Category-II CPSEs. Publishes the annual Public Enterprises Survey for Parliament.

New PSE Policy (2021, Atmanirbhar Bharat) — defines 4 strategic sectors (national/energy security, critical infra, financial services, minerals); non-strategic PSEs considered for privatisation or closure.

NLMC (National Land Monetisation Corporation) — 100% GoI-owned, incorporated 3 June 2022; monetises CPSEs' surplus/non-core land & buildings, including loss-making CPSEs' assets.

Department of Revenue, CBDT & GST 2.0

Department of Revenue oversees CBDT (direct taxes) and CBIC (GST/customs/excise). CBDT created under the Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963.

NextGen GST Reforms 2.0 — approved by GST Council 3-4 Sep 2025; simplified two-slab structure (5% & 18%), effective 22 Sep 2025; luxury/"sin goods" (tobacco etc.) retained at existing rates (tobacco fixed at 40%). GST taxpayer base grew from 66.5 lakh (2017) to 1.51 crore (2025); FY 2024-25 gross GST collections: ₹22.08 lakh crore (record, CAGR 18%).

Income Tax Reforms: Union Budget 2025-26 — incomes up to ₹12 lakh exempt (₹12.75 lakh effective for salaried, with standard deduction) under the new regime. New Income Tax Act, 2025 (announced Jul 2024) replaces the Income Tax Act, 1961 — introduces a unified "Tax Year" concept replacing Assessment Year/Previous Year. ITRs filed rose from 6.72 crore (FY21) to 9.19 crore (FY25) — up 36% in 5 years.

Exam angle: GST was introduced on 1 July 2017; the 2025 reform is called "GST 2.0" — don't confuse the original launch date with the 2025 rate rationalisation date (22 Sep 2025).

DIPAM

DIPAM (Department of Investment and Public Asset Management) — manages Central Government equity in CPSEs, strategic disinvestment, Offer for Sale (OFS). In FY 2025-26, facilitated stake sales in Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders, Bank of Maharashtra, Indian Overseas Bank.
